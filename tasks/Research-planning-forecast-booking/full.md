Finish the desk's planning notebook and leave it in a state the campaigns behind this one can run unchanged. The pack takes one sheet out of it, and the sheet is for the next shift the campaign runs that has no settled order against it: what each of that shift's four windows will carry in each of the two areas, and the van order the desk puts in for it.

`drops_fcst` is the number the engine put out for a zone row. `drops_fcst_adj` is what the engine smooths that column to across the shifts either side of it, and on a week whose shift levels move about as much as this one's do, a smoothed column is not that shift's own number. Read `drops_fcst`.

`as_of_shift` does not mean the same thing on the two sheets. One area's engine cuts its sheet before the shift and stamps the row with the shift it cut on, so its rows for a shift carry the stamp of the shift before it; the other stamps the row with the shift the row is about. Which engine does which is not the same from one campaign to the next, and neither sheet says. The shifts already settled say it: for each area, take that area's own metered drops on the settled shifts against the two readings the stamps allow, and use the reading the whole settled week sits closest to. One area's rows are then one shift behind the other's for the same shift, and every zone that engine emits carries that same stamp.

A zone that changed depot inside the campaign sits on both engines' sheets from the change on. The engine it left was never re-pointed and keeps emitting it every shift; the area it moved to added it to its own coverage sheet on the date. Count that zone once, in whichever area the routing table gives it for the shift you are ordering. Take that from the routing table against the shift itself, never against the stamp on the row: on the sheet that is cut early those two are a shift apart, so keying the change to the stamp leaves the shift the transfer lands on counted twice.

The zone rows are the number. The network's own line is a check on them and nothing else: it arrives per area and per shift, rounded to ten, with no window split at all, so it cannot give a window. Where the network line and the zone rows added up disagree, it is the zone rows that get read again.

Now the range under each number. A zone's own forecast miss is its own, and those wash out once the whole network is added up. The merchant promotion calendar does not wash out: it moves every zone of both areas the same way on the same shift. So take one reading of that move for each settled shift — the whole network's metered drops over what the sheets said for that shift — and the spread of those readings is what a fresh shift can still do to the desk. The range on a number is two of those spreads either side of it, low end first, and never tighter than the meter's own five-drop rounding. The whole network's range on its single line is two of those spreads either side of the network total, and because the calendar moves the eight windows together rather than each on its own, it is not the eight windows' ranges narrowed against one another.

Now the order. A van clears the number of drops the carrier agreement gives, in one window — the agreement's number, not eighteen. A booked van is charged whether the drops turn up or not, and a window that runs short is charged the agreement's multiple of one, which is why the order does not sit on the number. Every area and window carries at least one van, because the carrier will not run a window empty. After that a van costs one van-hour and saves the spill it prevents, so read a window's chance of running short off the same spread the range is built from, put each next van wherever it prevents the most, and stop adding when the allowance left is gone or when the next van would prevent less than it costs. That leaves the order high in the range rather than on the number.

The carrier holds forty vans for any one area and window, takes one order a shift covering both areas and all four windows, will not put a shift on the road under the minimum van-hours in the agreement, and reserves no further ahead than the fourteen days this calendar runs end to end. The quarter's allowance is the agreement's grant less everything the ledger has already been charged, which is 520 van-hours on this campaign, and a new order's standing van-hours have to fit inside it. An order the carrier hands back is charged the desk fee off the allowance all the same, and spill lands at settlement rather than at the order, so the allowance to plan against is the one the ledger leaves. When what is left will not carry the carrier's minimum there is nothing to order, and the answer is a stop.

Every order this campaign has been charged for appears in the pack once, with its order id, the shift it covers and the van-hours on it. That includes the one the carrier handed back: nothing ran, the desk fee was charged, and finance closes the quarter off that list.

`analysis.py` and `support.py` are the files to change. `protocol.py` is the shape the reporting stack reads a notebook through and ships with the stack. The campaigns behind this one run through the same two functions on their own zones, their own shift calendars, their own routing tables, their own carrier terms and their own allowances, and one of them has both engines stamping the row with the shift it is about and no zone on two rosters at all, so nothing in the notebook should be written for this week's numbers.
