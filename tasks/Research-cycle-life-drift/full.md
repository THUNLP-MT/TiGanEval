What the programme needs is one quantity: how many more cycles a cell reaches under the stepped charge than under the standard charge, at the temperature the build runs at, which is 25 C. The build changes to the stepped charge only if that is worth at least a hundred cycles; otherwise it keeps the standard charge. A cell run in the warm chamber is not evidence about the build on its own, because the warm chamber is on the bench to age cells faster and nothing in this campaign says a charge buys the same cycles at 45 C as it does at 25 C.

A cell is retired at the first capacity check that reads below eighty percent of the 2.50 Ah rating, and the tester writes a check every fifty cycles.

Three things about this campaign are not the way I assumed they were when I first read the export.

The tester's control software was updated on 12 May, and again on 3 August, and the capacity readings moved with it. The sealed reference cell is certified at 2.000 Ah and does not age, and it is read at the top of every session; since 12 May it has come back high, and by a different amount again since August. Everything the tester wrote in a session sits on whatever scale that session's reference reading shows, so cycle lives taken off sessions on different software are not the same measurement until they are put back on the certified cell's scale. Do that before anything is compared or averaged, and do it for a session we run today as well, because today's software is not the software of May.

The rack has a north bay held at 25 C and a south bay held at 45 C, and the channels a session used are what say which chamber its cells were in. The south chamber's controller was swapped on 14 May and its temperature trace has read the north probe ever since, so the temperature written on a session receipt after that date is not where those cells were sitting. The setpoints have not moved.

The charges were never spread evenly across the two bays. Whichever bay had channels free took the next fixture, so the stepped charge landed mostly in the north bay and the standard charge mostly in the south, and we have never run the standard charge in the north bay at all. That means the archive on its own cannot give the number the programme wants. Say so plainly rather than putting a figure on the board: report the difference at 25 C only when there are cells of both charges at 25 C, and otherwise report nothing for it and give the range instead.

Whatever the notebook does report, I need to know how far it could still move. Give the range the difference could be in given the cells we hold and the vendor's certificate, which binds every cell in this lot to a 25 C life between 600 and 1000 cycles under any charge in the qualification set. A fixture that has run is a measurement of the cells in it and the certificate is what stands in for a fixture that has not run, so the range is what those two leave open between them rather than a spread put around a figure we already have: where both charges have run at 25 C it closes on the difference itself, and where one of them has not the certificate is what holds the other end. It should be no wider than that and no narrower, because the office books six weeks of requalification work against it. The call, switch or keep, follows from that range against the hundred cycles: switch when its whole range is at least the bar, keep only when its whole range is below the bar, and nothing at all while both calls are still open.
