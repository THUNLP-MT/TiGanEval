Complete the broker channel so that the notification queues stay accountable under failure, expiry, pressure and restart. The drills recorded under `project_records/` are examples of what the queues do, not an exhaustive list of the situations the finished broker has to handle.

Every copy of a message the broker accepts ends in exactly one place. A copy is resident — waiting in one queue, or held by one worker under one delivery tag — or it is settled exactly once through `BrokerState.settle`: never both, and never twice. A copy that leaves a queue for a reason other than acknowledgement is settled with that reason; when its queue has a `dead_letter_queue` the disposition is `dead_lettered` and a copy arrives in that queue, and when it has none the disposition is `dropped`. The copy that arrives in the dead-letter queue carries the payload it was published with and the history of what took it out of its previous queue, which `record_death` writes: one entry per queue and reason with a count, plus first-death fields that agree with the oldest entry and are never rewritten.

Redelivery is bounded per queue. Rejecting a delivery with requeue puts the copy back at the head of the queue it came from and records that rejection; the queue's `max_deliveries` bounds how many times one copy may be handed out of that queue, and the rejection that would take it past the bound dead-letters the copy instead of putting it back. Rejecting without requeue dead-letters it immediately. Once a rejection has dead-lettered a copy, whether by reaching the bound or by refusing the requeue, that copy is never handed out of the queue it left again. Handouts are counted per queue, so `redelivery_count` for an in-flight delivery is how many times that copy was handed out of the queue it is in now before this one: the first handout out of any queue, the dead-letter queue it has just moved into included, reports zero.

A deadline is set when a copy is published: the publisher's `ttl` when one is given and the queue's `message_ttl` otherwise, measured from the clock reading at publication. A queue's `message_ttl` is the default for the copies nobody asks for a lifetime for, not a ceiling on what a publisher may ask, so a publish that names a longer lifetime than the queue's gets that longer one. One publish to two queues therefore produces two independent deadlines. A copy at or past its deadline is never handed to a worker. `get` skips it, dead-letters it with reason `expired` and moves on to the next copy; `drain_expired` removes and dead-letters exactly the ready copies that are past their deadline and returns how many it removed; and when the operator stops, no copy past its deadline is still waiting anywhere. A copy that moves into a dead-letter queue becomes a fresh resident of it and takes that queue's own deadline, which `dead_letter_copy` computes.

A queue with a `max_length` never holds more ready copies than that, at any point and not merely at the end. Whatever would take it over — a publish, a copy moving in from another queue, or a rejected delivery going back into it — the copy that has been waiting in that queue longest is evicted and settled with reason `maxlen`, so on-call can still find it and see what took it out; it is dead-lettered when that queue has a `dead_letter_queue` and dropped when it has none. How long a copy has been waiting is its own `published_at` and not where it sits in the queue: a rejection puts a copy back at the front, so the copy at the front can be younger than the ones behind it, and the eviction still takes the oldest of the copies waiting there. A copy a worker is holding is not waiting in the queue and is never the victim, and an arrival into one queue never changes the contents of another.

A restart rebuilds `BrokerState` from `snapshot()` and constructs a new `Channel`; anything the channel object kept on itself is gone. Ready copies, in-flight deliveries, dead-letter contents, the ledger and each copy's rejection history all survive it. An acknowledged copy never comes back, a queue with live work waiting in it hands that work out immediately afterwards, and a copy that has used all but one of its queue's `max_deliveries` is dead-lettered by its next rejection even when the restart falls between them.

`broker/channel.py` and `broker/support.py` are the files to change. `broker/protocol.py` is shared with the worker pool and the operator console. You can inspect the project, replay the recorded drills and run your own experiments before you deliver.
