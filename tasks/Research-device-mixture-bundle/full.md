Finish the Harrowgate bundle pack and hand it back as a notebook the next depot can run unchanged. The board takes one decision from it: which of the three bundles goes on the whole handheld train, and what it costs at the depot it serves worst.

The fleet is what the register says is in service, not whoever happened to get the capture app. The app reached a hundred and fifty-two units of the seven hundred and eighty on the register, and it reached them because those crews would keep it charged, not because they are the fleet in miniature. Two thirds of the export is counter work and the register is barely two fifths counter units. The reference card is a target on a stand: bench rows tell you what a unit writes, and they are not captures from a bay, so nothing about the fleet's number is read off them.

Every unit on the register carries the bay it works in and the mixture of conditions it captures in. Units in the same bay capture alike, and units in different bays do not: a counter unit never sees a dim or a damaged label and a yard unit is three quarters glare. So a bay's number is what that bay's own rows say, and rows from another bay say nothing about it. Rows the desk sends back belong to the bay the unit they came off works in, and they join that bay's rows for the number and for the range alike.

The number the board takes is the bundle's points of error at the bay it serves worst — not its average over the fleet, and not one rate over the whole export. One decimal, lower better, and the pack has to carry every bundle's number that way as well as the one to ship, because the representative reads all three. Ship the bundle whose worst bay costs least. To within two points, here and at Ledbury, Ashfield and Penhale behind it.

A unit takes its own capture gain off a capture before a bundle sees it, so the number that matters is the one the unit would have got. The exporter does not do that for you: it writes captures from units on 4.2 as they left the sensor, with the raised gain still on the three reflectance windows, and writes captures from earlier firmware corrected. The `stage` column says which is which on every row, and the same is true of rows the desk sends back. Put a raised row back on the unit's own scale before you score it, and score it through the vendor's own scorer, because the decision the exporter recorded against that row is the decision the bundle would have made on the raised numbers. The gain is a property of the firmware and not of the bay, so there is nothing to carry into a fleet that never took 4.2.

Because the export is one draw of units and one state of the rollout, the same fleet has to give the same bundle and the same number when the export is written again off a different set of units or a different rollout. Anything read off the export as a whole moves with which bays the app was on; anything read off raised rows as they sit moves with how far the rollout had gone.

Print a range under the number, low end first, and make it the one that holds nineteen times in twenty. It is a statement about the rows behind the bay that decides — that bundle's worst bay — so it is what those rows leave open and nothing else: no tighter, because that claims rows you do not have, and no looser, because the board staffs against it. Say which bay it is and how many rows stand behind it.

For the next request: the campaign was granted 1,200 credits and has 920 left. One request names one bay, one firmware, one condition and at most 120 rows, and the desk will not split it. Counter rows cost one credit each and every other bay costs three, the reference card costs one on either firmware, and a request the desk turns away still costs twenty. Name a cell the register has units in — that bay, on that firmware, seeing that condition — or the desk hands it back and bills the twenty for nothing.

What a request is worth is how much narrower it leaves the top two bundles, and the request to beat is the one already in the notebook: a full counter request. Rows in a bay that decides neither of the top two buy nothing at all. Stop when what is left cannot buy the smallest request the desk takes, which is twenty rows, or when the top two already stand further apart than the range under the number. Return that stop rather than a request the campaign cannot use.

Every request the desk billed appears in the pack once, with its receipt id, the rows it returned and the credits it charged, the two it handed back included and marked on their line as handed back, and the total the pack gives has to be the total on the receipt file. A line the receipts do not carry is not a request.

`analysis.py` and `support.py` are the files to change. `protocol.py` is the shape the release stack reads a notebook through and ships with the stack, and `bundles/` is the vendor's scorer. The same notebook runs Ledbury, Ashfield and Penhale on their own registers, their own capture reach, their own gains and rollout dates, and their own grants, and one fleet behind them works every bay the same way and never took 4.2 at all. There the bays do not pull a bundle's number apart the way they do here: the bay a bundle does worst at, one rate over the whole of its export, and the bays put together at the register's unit counts all land within two points of each other, and mallow is the bundle to ship on any of them. Its grant is nearly gone, with less left than the smallest request the desk takes, so the answer for the next capture is a stop with credits still in hand. Nothing in the notebook should be written for Harrowgate's numbers.
