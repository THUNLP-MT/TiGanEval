Finish the incident console's rich pane so that an on-call can read it during a page. The sessions recorded under `project_records/` are examples of what the console does to a pane, not an exhaustive list of the situations the finished pane has to handle.

While the reader is not on the newest line the pane holds their place. The entry at the top of the viewport stays at the top when workers append, when `max_entries` drops the oldest entries off the head, and when the divider or the reading minimum changes what every entry is drawn as. While that entry comes out of `render_entry` exactly as it did before the event, the reader stays on the same line inside it: a divider that moved a column or two, or a reading minimum that changed without changing where that entry's words fall, leaves the line they were on carrying the same words, so it is still their place even where every other entry around it was broken differently. The console's house policy uses the held entry's complete rendered strip as the boundary: if any row of that strip changes, the top settles on the entry's first line, the one with its time and its worker on it, even when the particular row the reader had occupied happens to contain the same words after the redraw. It is the entry's drawing that decides which of the two happens, not which control the operator touched. Where the cap has dropped the entry she was holding, the pane falls back to the oldest entry it still holds; and where the log has shrunk so far that the held line cannot sit at the top any more, the pane sits at the end. None of this applies while the reader is on the newest line: there the pane follows the newest line, which is what the console wants during an incident.

`is_following_end` is the viewport's position and nothing else: after every event it equals `at_end(scroll_y, max_scroll_y)`, where `max_scroll_y` is the bound `protocol.max_scroll_y` gives for the display lines the pane is showing and the height it was built with. It comes back on its own when the reader scrolls or drags down to the newest line with no button pressed, and it comes back when the content shrinks under a stationary viewport until the newest line is on screen — a reading minimum that puts more text on each row, entries dropped by the cap, a cleared pane. `follow_end()` sets the position to the end of the log the pane is drawing and lets the state fall out of it.

The pane posts exactly one `FollowChanged` for each actual change of that state, and nothing at all for an update that leaves it as it was, however many entries the batch wrote. Transitions the reader did not cause count: an append, a prune, a resize or a change of the reading minimum that moves the pane on to or off the newest line is a change like any other. Each record carries the pane's name and the `scroll_y` and `max_scroll_y` the pane settled on after that event rather than the ones read before it, so replaying the session audit reconstructs the sequence the badge showed.

Every entry on the pane is drawn as `render_entry(text, width, min_width, full_width)` gives it at the pane's current width and reading minimum. That holds for entries written before the layout gave the pane a width, which belong on screen at the first width the layout settles on with their full-width flag intact; for entries already on screen when an operator drags the divider or changes the reading minimum; and for the rules and banners the console writes full width, which reach both edges at whatever width the pane is now. Keeping the entries and drawing them when they are read is what makes that possible; a strip drawn once at write time stops being the text the exporter writes.

The extent the pane reports is the extent of what is on it now. The line count is the display lines of the entries the pane currently holds, and the widest line is the widest of those lines, recomputed over what is left after a prune and after a redraw rather than carried forward as the widest line ever drawn or narrowed to the rows that happen to be on the screen. Both scrollbars are sized from it, so a widest line that only grows leaves the reader dragging into empty space long after the entry that caused it has gone.

`console/rich_pane.py` and `console/support.py` are the files to change. `console/protocol.py` ships in the console release and the incident exporter reads the same file. You can inspect the project, replay the recorded sessions and run your own experiments before you deliver.
