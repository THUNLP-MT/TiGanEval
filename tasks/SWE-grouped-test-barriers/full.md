Finish the device lab's night runner so that the handsets on one bench work a case at the same time without treading on each other, and so that a night that goes wrong costs the lab that bench rather than the fleet. The recorded nights under `project_records/` are examples of what the lab runs, not an exhaustive list of the sessions the finished runner has to survive.

The morning report holds exactly one row per case per handset for every bench that opened, and no row at all for a bench that did not. Every `flag` a running step writes goes on the row of the run that wrote it; ordinary `note` steps remain in the console trace. A case runs once on each handset of the bench, so a flag about the phone it is driving lands on that phone's line and on no other, and no other handset's line for that case carries it. That means the record a step writes to belongs to the execution taking its turn rather than to the runner, because two handsets are working through the same case body at once and whichever of them started last must not own the pen for both.

A named point is not its name. It belongs to the one meeting that reached it — the phase or case in hand, and the handsets due there — and a handset leaves it only when that meeting's arrivals are complete. The moment the last of them passes through, the meeting is finished and gone, so the next use of the same name is a new meeting: later in the same case body, in a later case, or on a bench that runs after this one. A body that stops twice at the same name stops twice, and a name one bench has already finished with never lets the next bench's phones straight through.

How many handsets a named point waits for is read from the bench that owns the execution reaching it, as that bench stands in the session in hand rather than in the inventory. A point reached inside a case waits for every handset of that bench. A bench's own opening and closing phases are what the bench does as a whole: each runs once for the bench, driven with one handset in hand rather than repeated over the bench's phones, so a point reached from one of them has a single handset to wait for and must return rather than block. Nothing counts arrivals against the whole session: benches differ in size, and a phone waiting for handsets that are not on its bench parks until morning, which costs the lab a night rather than a test. When a night ends, no handset is left parked at a named point.

`current_device`-style bindings live no longer than the phase that claimed a handset. `harness/protocol.py` names the phases that own one; inside them a step is told the handset of the execution taking its turn, and outside them the question has no answer and raises the suite error the contract builds, on every exit path including the one a failing phase takes. A request for a named point from a phase that has claimed no handset raises the suite error naming the entry point, and it is `protocol.check_gate_request` that decides all of this, including the split the pager rota rests on: a wait that cannot exist because it is negative is a `ValueError` in the author's arithmetic, and a wait of zero is a suite error, and the two must not be collapsed into one.

The night's own opening runs exactly once, before any bench takes a handset, and its closing runs exactly once at the end whatever happened in between — after a bench that failed, after a night in which every case failed. A bench whose opening raises or refuses runs none of its cases, still runs its closing, and does not stop the benches after it. A case that ends badly costs that case and no more, whichever way it ended: a handset that stops answering, and a step the bench controllers refuse outright — a named point asked for with a wait that cannot exist — both leave the bench's later cases, the bench's closing and every bench behind it exactly where they were, with that case's rows still in the report. Every handset a bench takes out of the pool goes back into it once, on every way out, because a handset still booked out in the morning is a handset the next night's session cannot get.

`harness/sequencing.py` and `harness/rendezvous.py` are the files to change. `harness/protocol.py` ships with the bench controllers. You can inspect the workspace, replay the recorded nights and run your own experiments before you deliver.
