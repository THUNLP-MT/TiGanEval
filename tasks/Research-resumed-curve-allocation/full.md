The Friday review takes two things out of it: which run the node-hours we have left should buy, and the loss that run reaches once they have been spent on it, to within four hundredths, with the range printed under it.

The number is about where a run gets to, not where it is now. Every run on `run_ledger.csv` is in scope, because the hours could go to any of them, and the loss is the one the trainer writes. Reckon the hours on the fastest node type the window still has free, since that is where they would actually be booked. All of them, and all of them on the one run: the number answers what the whole of what is left buys on the run being funded, and the runs are put in order against each other at that same reach, because which of them comes out lowest depends on how far past their own rows they are read. The booking at the end of this splits those hours between two runs to settle the choice; that is what the window does next, and it does not shorten the reach the number above it is read at.

The step column starts again at zero every time a run is resumed. `first_step` is nought on every row of the ledger and `resumed_from` chains the segments together, so the training a run has actually had is its segments added up along that chain, and its last row sits at whatever step its own last segment happened to reach rather than at the training behind it. Nothing writes down what a node type turns a wall hour into either; its own segments do, their `last_step` against their `wall_hours`, and the console's receipts say the same for the jobs it ran.

A run improves along a power of the training it has had, towards a floor it does not go below, and it is that curve the review is asking about. A row the trainer writes is not a point on it. It sits over the level by an amount that goes with the learning rate the row was written at — most of it just after a restart, next to nothing at the bottom of a decay — and on this window that amount is bigger than the gap the board shows between the two runs at the top of it. It is the same instrument at every run and the `lr` column says where every row stands in it, so carry that column alongside the curve rather than leaving it in the scatter. A pause point is therefore not a level, two runs cannot be told apart on their last rows, and what the review wants is where a run's curve sits at the training it will have had, not what a row would print there: the curve with the whole of that amount taken back off it, rather than the curve read at whatever rate the run's own rows happened to be written at.

cfg-05 went out on the old launch script and its hook writes a row every 2,000 steps instead of every 250. That does two things and only two: its last row sits further behind the end of its last segment than anybody else's, and there are far fewer rows behind its curve. Neither of them is a reason to think less of the run.

The range goes under the number, low endpoint first and high endpoint second, with the number itself between them, and it is a range for where that run's curve sits once the hours are on it rather than for what the next row the trainer writes there would print. It is what the rows behind the funded run's own curve leave open once the curve is drawn through them: how far those rows scatter about it, how many of them there are, and how far past the last of them the number is being read. It has to carry how far the shape of that curve is itself still open at that reach as well, not the scatter about it alone, because on a run whose rows stop early that is most of what is open. Those go in as one reading of how tightly the rows hold the curve where the number is read, not as allowances laid one on top of another: a row's own scatter put around the number on top of that is the next-row band again, whatever else has gone in with it. Say it so that it would have been safe at any of the windows this slide is made for: the review makes it on this window and on the six after it, and the one time in twenty it allows itself to be wrong is over that whole run of them rather than at each one of them, so a range built for a single reading is narrower than that allows. Nothing tighter than what those rows leave open, because the review commits on it; no wider than eight hundredths end to end, because a range the review argues about is not an answer; and nothing several times wider than what the rows leave open either, because a band that wide is the cap written down as an allowance rather than a reading of the run.

Then the booking. The window was granted fifty node-hours and eight have been charged, so forty-two are left; `collection_receipts.json` is the record, a job that runs is charged the hours it ran and a job the console turns away is charged one hour, and both sit on the stream. `n-a` is held for the evaluation team for the whole window, so the only hours that exist are `n-b` and `n-c`. A job runs four wall hours at the least and sixteen at the most, on a run the ledger carries.

The hours are only worth spending where they change the answer, and that is the two runs that land lowest at the reach the number above was read at: the one you are funding and the one behind it. Put a job on each of those two and give them the same number of steps as each other, which is the same hours when they are on the same node type and is not when they are not. Book them on the node type the number was reckoned on, since that is the training the number assumes those hours buy. Hours on one of them alone separate nothing, and hours on a third run separate nothing either. That makes eight hours the smallest booking worth making, so a window with fewer than eight left is one to stop on, and a window still holding eight is not finished.

Every segment on these ledgers belongs to the cumulative curve of its run and goes into the number, so every one appears in the note once as used, with no set-aside reason. Every receipt on the stream appears once, the turned-away ones included with the hour each cost, and its run, node, charged hours and returned steps are copied as the stream records them. The hours the note gives have to be the hours the stream gives, and what it says is left has to be the grant less those.
