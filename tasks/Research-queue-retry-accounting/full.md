Finish the September failure review on `/v1/quote` and hand it back as a notebook the next review can run unchanged. Finance takes one number from it: of the customer operations the route served in the review window, what share ended with no successful attempt. An operation is one thing a customer asked us for, however many calls we made on their behalf, so the number is not a share of the calls in the log and not the percentage the dashboard draws.

The client library is what makes those two different. Up to 2.4.0 the client wrote one receipt when it was done with an operation, with no attempt number and no retryable flag on it. From 2.4.0 it writes a receipt for every call it makes, carrying the attempt number and the flag the server sent back. Both shapes carry the version of the client that wrote them, and the export writes that version as `2.3` on the old shape and `2.4` on the new one, so no receipt in the log is missing it. Each receipt says which shape it is in its own `client_version` field, and that field is what to read, not the calendar: the four fleets went over in two groups a night apart, so the evening between the two moves carries both shapes in the same minutes and a rule keyed on the day of the week gets that evening wrong. The reviews on the share drive crossed over on their own evenings, and one of them never finished crossing over at all.

An operation ended with no successful attempt when a call it made failed for something the client would not retry, wherever in the chain that call came, or when the client ran out of attempts. The number of attempts the client makes is a deployment setting, and it is not the same setting in every window on that share drive. Read the cap off the receipts in front of you rather than fixing it at the three this window's changelog gives. At the last attempt the client makes, a failure ends the operation whatever the retryable flag on it says.

The four edge pools send probes to the same route through the same client. Their probes normally go to the pool's own sink, but when a pool runs past its threshold a share of that minute's probes is written into the customer log instead, and there a probe looks exactly like a customer's first call. `edge_pool_counters.json` says how many spilled, pool by pool and minute by minute, and it never says how they ended. Take those probes off the operation count in the minutes they spilled into, using that minute's own counters, because the spill lands in some minutes and not others.

The pools run with retry switched off and on a short deadline, so a spilled probe that came back in time is written among the successes and one that expired is written as a first call the client would not retry. That means the expired probes are sitting inside the first calls marked not retryable, mixed with real customer failures, and no counter says how many. Two things pin some of them down. Where every first call on the new client in a minute was a probe, because no customer fleet had reached the new client yet, the export already says how those probes ended. And every edge trace unit already spent returns the probe count and the expiry count for the pool-minute it covers.

For the minutes still unpinned, give finance a range: the low end has every one of those spilled probes expiring, the high end has none of them expiring, and both ends are worked out minute by minute. In one minute the expired probes can never be more than the first calls that minute wrote as not retryable, and that ceiling is a different number in every minute, so a single ceiling taken across the whole window admits allocations the counters already rule out. The window is every operation the route served in that block rather than a sample drawn from it, so that spread is the whole of the range and there is nothing to add for how far a sample might have landed from the truth. Report the range low end first, with the number you are giving finance inside it.

Where inside that range the number goes is set by the probes whose ends are already known. Take the share of them that expired, over the minutes the export settles by itself and the pool-minutes the spent units cover together, and give the probes nobody has pulled that same share, minute by minute and never more of them in a minute than that minute's own ceiling leaves. The report carries the count of operations that ended with no successful attempt beside that share and the operation count it is out of, and all three are the one reading: the count is what the number we are giving finance is made of, and not either end of the range.

Platform grants twelve trace units a month, one per pool-minute, and they do not roll over; five are spent. The edge archive holds raw pool rows for 72 hours and drops the rest every night, so the two evenings I was paged are out of reach whatever we spend. Point the next pull at a minute the archive still holds where probes spilled and nobody has pulled that pool yet, because that is the only kind of pull that narrows the range. Stop when no such minute is left, and stop as well when the month's units are gone even with minutes still unpinned, because a pull the review cannot pay for is not a plan.

Every trace unit this review was billed for appears in the report once, with its minute, its pool and the two counts that unit returned, including the one spent on an off-peak minute where nothing had spilled. A line the receipt ledger does not carry is not a measurement.
