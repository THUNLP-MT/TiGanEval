Finish the Halcombe rollout readout and hand it back as a notebook the next network can run unchanged. The board takes one decision from it: batching goes to the last five metros, or Halcombe stays on the one-drop dispatcher.

The number is about the region book cut on 1 February and about the metros on it that went live inside the window closing on 20 April, and about nothing else. A metro the book does not carry is not in the readout even if it went live in the window, and a metro on the book that went live after the window closes has nothing to put in it yet. Each metro that is in it enters at the orders the book counted for it on the day the book was cut, which is what `region_book.json` carries; that is the size the board committed the half against. A region-week booked now is not for this readout at all — it serves the next board.

Report the move in on-time share as batching minus no batching, in on-time points, to one decimal, positive meaning batching helped. Read each metro over its first 28 days on batching and no further: the depot managers signed up on their first four weeks, and a metro that has been live for three months would otherwise carry three times the weight of one that went live in April. Report the metro-days that reading is built on as well; the board's own count of what the number stands on.

On-time has to mean one thing across the whole window, and in `region_daily.csv` it does not. `ontime_orders` is written by whichever dispatch service the metro is on, and the two services count from different stamps: the one-drop dispatcher from `placed_at`, the batching stack from `assigned_at`. A metro moves between them on its `stack_migrated_on` date, and the printed rate steps several points on that date whether or not batching ever went live there — Vell's steps on 13 January and Vell is not on the rollout list at all. So do not read a rate off `ontime_orders`. `order_sample.csv` carries both counts over the same sampled orders for every metro-day of the window, so pick one of the two stamps and read every rate as that stamp's on-time count over that metro-day's sampled orders, on both sides of every migration date and on every metro. A metro's four weeks is the average of those metro-day rates, one day one reading, not one count over the whole stretch: the sample is a few percent of a day's orders and the big days would otherwise speak for the small ones.

Read a metro's 28 days against the 28 days before it went live, and read the comparison metros over the same two stretches of calendar. When the export begins inside that before-period, use every pre-live day it carries; do not drop the metro or any of its batching days to manufacture 28 day-matched pairs. The comparison is the metros in that metro's own service band that had not gone live yet on the day being read, whether or not the book carries them — the book decides what the number is about, not what it is read against — and it is those same metros, the ones its band still had waiting on the day being read, that are read back over its four weeks before — the comparison does not change membership between the two stretches. The band is the drift, and two metros in one band move together while two in different bands do not. A metro that is already running batching is carrying the thing being measured, so it is not a comparison, however clean its rows look — and by April most of the band is already live, so the comparison shrinks as the rollout goes on. That is the shape of the thing; do not fill it back up.

Riverbend counts the seven days it actually ran. The on-call log has batching disabled there at 09:40 on 11 March and it never came back, so from that morning Riverbend is on neither side: not batching, and not a comparison for anyone else either. It is the largest metro the window puts in the readout, so counting its three quiet weeks as batching pulls the whole number down by most of a point.

Say how far the number could still be from where it lands, as a range with the low endpoint first. The width comes from the sampled orders behind the metro-days the reading is built on and from nothing else — the metro-day counts are what they are, and the thin ones, a small comparison metro in a small band, are most of the width. Two things fix how wide. It has to be a range that would have been safe on all four of the networks this notebook produces a readout for, not one that is right one time in twenty on each of them separately, because delivery finance's sentence is one chance in twenty across the whole set; and it must not be narrower than those sampled orders leave open — a range printed at the board's own bar without reading the rows is not a measurement of anything. And it must not be wider than those sampled orders support either: a range that hedges by taking the width the rows give and multiplying it is telling the board less than the rows already tell it.

The board acts when the range is inside 1.5 points, and on this window it is nowhere near, which is exactly why there is capacity left to spend. So the answer to what to book next is a stop only when the range this readout printed is already inside the bar, or when there is no region-week the wave desk will serve; anything else, and the capacity goes back unspent.

What the wave desk will serve is in the standing minute. Twelve region-weeks were granted and two are charged, so ten are left. At most three region-weeks in any one calendar week, and only the weeks the board is still selling. Between 20 and 40 percent of the metro's orders held back from batching for the week — that band is Halcombe's, and the desk serves every network on its own terms — not zero, because a week with nothing held back has nothing to compare against and tells the next board nothing. Only a metro that has not gone live by the end of that week, and only one running more than 400 orders a day. A week the board hands back is charged against the twelve all the same. If you need one new week to test the notebook before delivery, you may book at most one of the remaining region-weeks; `collect` is a real charged booking, not a rehearsal.

A booked week measures one metro against its own held-back orders for seven days, and what it is worth to the next board is what the thinner of its two arms carries and how much of the book that metro is. Tova's own plan is the week to beat: the largest metro the desk will still serve, the smallest held-back share the minute allows, a full week. Anything you book has to leave the next board's range at least as narrow as that would.

Every region-week the receipts carry appears in the readout exactly once, with its receipt id, the region, the week, the region-weeks it was charged and the days it returned. That includes the week the board handed back, which returned no days and was charged anyway. A week the receipts do not carry is not a measurement and does not belong in the readout.
