Finish the misroute review and leave it as a notebook whoever runs the next one can run unchanged. The board takes one figure off it: the share of the tickets we received in the review window that the classifier put in the wrong queue.

That share is about the tickets in the intake export, first day to last, and about nothing else. The file the vendor labels from is a March export of this desk and its channels sit in nothing like the proportions last month's post did; the items somebody chose to send the vendor are further off again, because the sampler takes what the classifier is least sure of and each receipt records the chance its item had of being taken. Neither of those two sets of tickets is the month we are reviewing.

The queue a ticket belongs in follows from its segment. Every ticket carrying a segment belongs in the same queue as every other ticket on that segment, whatever the classifier did with it, so one label on a segment settles that segment for the whole export. The classifier reads the wording rather than the segment, so inside one segment it sends some tickets to that queue and the rest to one neighbouring queue, and which of the two it picked is on every row of both exports.

The vendor gives a session to one reviewer, who works the list from the top. For a stretch at the head of a session the labels that come back are the segment's queue. Past that the reviewer stops opening the ticket and confirms the routing the vendor's console has already filled in, which is our own classifier's answer, and the chance of that climbs with the position in the session. The sheet the senior agents settled is what shows where the stretch ends: it reaches items from the head of a sitting to the back of one, though only across the part of the catalogue the panel owned, and the first position at which a settled item and the vendor's label for it part company is past the end of the stretch. The stretch is not a fixed number, because a reviewer who goes off the boil sooner has a shorter one, so the notebook has to read it off the settled sheet every time rather than carry a number of its own.

One stretch covers the whole account. The reviewer goes off at the same place whichever sitting is in front of them, so take the earliest position anywhere on the ledger where the sheet and a vendor label part company, and let every label before it stand: the sittings the panel worked through and the sittings it never opened settle a segment on the same terms, and a label the panel never looked at is the segment's queue if it came back before that position. When the sheet and the vendor agree on every item the sheet covers, nothing on that reading says the reviewer ever went off, and the labels stand as far down the list as the sittings went. The reviewer does not go off all at once, so a segment can come back with two answers from inside the stretch; where that happens its queue is the one more of its labels name, rather than whichever of them was read first or last.

A segment the account holds no label for from inside that stretch is a segment the review cannot say anything about, and the sampler has left us a set of them: it sends the vendor whatever the classifier is least sure of, so the segments it is confident about are the ones nobody has bought, and nothing says those are the quiet corners of the month. Placing that order is part of finishing this rather than something to hand on. Work out which segments they are, place an order from the pool that brings as much of that unread intake into view as the balance, dates and reliable stretch allow, wait for the receipts to land on the ledger, and read the figure off the account as it stands after that; this desk's balance covers all of them many times over. A figure worked out on an account that is still short of those labels is a figure about part of last month whatever it is written over, and the range around it is no better.

The segment tagger was down for the first days of the window, and the tickets from those days carry a product line and no segment. The failed enrichment did not erase the ticket ids or the source extract's batch boundaries. In ticket-id order each blank run is followed by the tagged remainder of the same batch, so put those rows back on that batch's segment rather than treating them as free to sit anywhere under the product line. Once the account has a reliable queue for every segment, every intake row is fixed and the two range endpoints meet at the reported share. The window is every ticket the desk received over those days rather than a sample drawn from them, so there is nothing to add for how far a sample might have landed from the truth.

Every receipt the vendor issued against this account appears in the review once, with the session it came from and the item it covers. A delivery that was retried put receipts the vendor had already issued onto the ledger a second time; those lines are the same purchase and are counted once. The credits the review reports have to be the credits on the vendor's statement.

The next order uses items the pool still holds on segments the desk still cannot read. Choose a feasible set that brings the largest reachable share of last month's intake into view; no particular item or listing order is privileged when two plans reach the same share. Keep a sitting short enough to stay inside the stretch, ask for no more sittings than there are days before the cut-off, and name only items the pool still holds that we have not already paid for. The whole order has to come out of the balance that is left, with the set-up charge counted again for every sitting in it, rather than each sitting weighed against the balance on its own. Work that balance out from the ledger in front of you — the credits contracted for the account less what the receipts on it come to, set-up charges counted the same way the report counts them — rather than off the used-and-left figures a statement prints, which were true of the day that statement was written and are neither the balance after a session of our own nor the balance on any other desk. A partial order that brings the largest reachable share into view is worth placing, and refusing to order at all because the whole list is out of reach leaves the month dark for nothing. When there is no segment left that the balance and the pool can still reach, stop: a session that buys labels where the desk can already say what the queue should be spends credits and changes nothing.
