Finish September's gate-pass readout and hand it back as a notebook the next month can run unchanged. The committee takes two things out of it: what one gate-pass ticket does to the account it was addressed to, to within eight tenths of a point, with the range printed under it; and what to book with the rest of the grant.

The number is what a pass does to the account whose name came out of the barrel. It is not what the campaign did to an estate and it is not what the month did to the office. Measure it against the accounts at addresses no ticket reached this month, not against every account that did not get one: a pass fitted a keypad tag to the door it went to, that tag opens for every account registered at the same address whether or not it was sent anything, and those neighbours are sitting in the uninvited row of my pivot lifting it. The tag belongs to the address, so `address_id` in the register is what says who shares one; the estate column is far too coarse for it and two accounts in the same estate share nothing.

The address is also the unit that moves, not the account. People at one address talk to each other and join together, so four accounts at one door are one reading and not four, and both sides of the comparison have to be counted that way when you say how far the number could sit from the truth. The range the committee reads under a figure is the one that holds nineteen times in twenty, and that is the one to print. It has to hold what a pass is really worth, and it must never be tighter than the addresses behind it support. Nothing looser either: the register is the whole office rather than a sample of it, so there is nothing to add on top of what the addresses leave open.

Sign-ups are the register's own `activated_at` stamps and nothing else. `signup_counter.csv` is written by the nightly job, and the total on a row is the register's total to two midnights back, or to the Friday before it where that midnight falls on a Saturday or a Sunday, so a drum that lands on a Friday is missing from the counter's row for that day and the one after it while the register already carries it. Count off the stamps, and the number will not move when the job runs late, catches up, or when a drum was posted a few days either way.

The mail house drops each name back in the barrel before it pulls the next one, so tickets printed is not accounts reached: two tickets to one account is one account and two charges, and a run of tickets always lands on fewer accounts than it prints. That is also what a booking is worth. A second pass to an account that already has one buys nothing at all, and neither does a pass to an account already stamped on the register, so a run is worth only what it does to the accounts that have not joined yet. A pass into an address whose door already carries a tag buys only the difference between what a pass is worth and what the tag was already worth there. A pass into an address no ticket has reached buys its own account and lifts everyone else living there, so a fresh address with several accounts on it is worth several times a name the campaign has already been to. What the tag is worth to those neighbours is a number of its own and a smaller one than what a pass is worth to the account it was addressed to, so read it off the register the same way that one is read — the accounts sitting behind a door a ticket reached, against the accounts at addresses no ticket reached — and price a run with each of the two at its own size rather than at the same one.

The committee set September at 1,000 sign-ups stamped on the register on or before the 29th, counted the same way. The account holds 3,180 credits, and a plan has to be one the mail house will actually print: inside the balance, priced at each drum's own credits per hundred rounded up to the hundred, at most 1,200 tickets off any one estate drum in the month, posted on or before the 27th, and never asking a barrel for more tickets than it has names left. The reserve the mail house holds back is against bookings the desk makes on its own account, and the month's remaining plan draws on the whole balance. A booking they turn back is charged the 40-credit setup and prints nothing. The office keeps the waitlist barrel's names on paper at the counter and nothing in the register says which accounts are still on it, so there is no sizing a booking against it and the barrels a plan is worked out over are the citywide one and the estate ones.

For planning, a run reaches the target when what it is expected to add leaves the month no more than a tenth of its current shortfall below the target, and never more than two sign-ups below it. Size against that line; the committee uses it because the value of a pass is itself an estimate. Among the plans that reach that line, do not hand back one that spends grossly more of the grant than another valid plan needs; where two come to the same, either will do. Where the register already stands at or above the target, book nothing more and say so as a stop rather than as an empty list.

Every booking the ledger carries appears in the readout once, with its receipt id, the tickets it printed, the accounts those tickets landed on and the credits it charged — the turned-back one included, at nought tickets and its setup. Both counts are printed and they are not the same number. The month's totals in the readout are the ledger's totals.

`report.py` and `support.py` are the files to change. `protocol.py` is the shape the reporting stack reads a notebook through and ships with the stack. The same notebook runs October here and the Brightmoor, Calder, Dunmoor, Everleigh, Fenwick and Garrowhill offices, each on its own register, its own drums, its own prices, its own nightly job, its own allowance and its own cut-off, and its own grant and target. One of those offices has one account to every address on its books, so no keypad tag there is ever shared and the uninvited accounts simply are the accounts at addresses no ticket reached; the comparison is the same comparison, there is nothing to correct, and a range built on addresses is a range built on accounts. Nothing in the notebook should be written for Ashwell's numbers.
