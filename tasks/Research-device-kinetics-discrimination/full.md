Finish the EM-9 March soak report and hand it back as a notebook the campaigns queued behind March can run unchanged. The review takes one number out of it: the hours a module of this build lasts before a tenth of its output has gone, at the drive and the case the specification is written against, which are 1.4 A and 85 °C. It goes in in hours and the reviewer's tolerance on it is 250 hours, with a range printed underneath.

The modules this campaign counts are the ones with bench rows in the export. One module on the incoming sheet was pulled for the metrology cross-check before it went on a rack, so it has no rows and no soak, and it is not one of them.

Where a module started is what the metrology bench read it at on the way in, which is `incoming_mw` on the incoming sheet, and not its first bench row. The bench meter stops at the sticker on its front, and every module in this family comes off the line above that sticker, so the opening rows of every trace read the sticker figure exactly. A row sitting there says the module was at least that bright and does not say where, so it carries no reading and belongs to no part of the curve. `REF-20` is the shop's reference source at 20 mW, read into the export at every session and well under the sticker, and it comes back honest, which is how you know the sticker rows are the meter and not the modules. Its own rows are not a module's rows.

Hours are wall clock. The export writes `elapsed_h` out of the logger's sample index at an hour a sample, and the logger on the rack that came back from the workshop writes two rows an hour, so that rack's column runs at twice its own clock. The `logged_at` stamps are what decide how long a module has been on, and they say what one step of the column is worth on each rack. It is the stamps against the column that decide it, and not which rack it happens to be, because the campaigns behind March had a different rack rebuilt or none at all.

Every law the catalogue carries goes to the corrected rows. Read a law one setting at a time: a setting is a drive and a case the campaign has rows at, and how far the law's curve sits from the rows at that one setting is the root-mean-square of what those rows and no others leave under it, in the meter's own milliwatts, which is the same thing the repeat spread is quoted in. That distance is taken again at each setting and never once over the rows pooled together, because a handful of rows at a corner the campaign has not run before would disappear into the hundreds at the settings it has already run. A law is left standing while every setting it is read at comes back inside half again the meter's repeat spread, counting only a setting with eight rows or more behind it: one setting over the bar puts the law down however well it sits at the rest and however few rows that setting holds. A law that never reaches a tenth at all on those rows is not standing either. More than one law stands on this build, which is the argument reliability has been having about EM-9 since the family opened. The report names the laws that stand, by the field names the catalogue uses, and the figure it hands the review is the middle of what they answer: the hours each standing law gives, averaged.

The range is printed low end first. It is never tighter than the standing laws leave it — that is, as far either side of the figure as their own answers are apart — and never tighter than three standard errors of the figure off the rows counted — that is, the figure moved by three standard errors of the one constant a law leaves free, taken off the scatter those rows leave in that constant — whichever of those two is larger. It is never wider than four times that larger one, and when only one law is left standing the second of the two is the whole of it. There is nothing to add on top and nothing to take off, because these are the modules that were run rather than a draw from a larger build, and the reviewer books the datasheet against the low end. The desk will not print a range wider than 350 hours, and March's is wider than that, which is why the review is still buying chamber time.

Now the chamber. `chamber_plan.md` has the terms: fourteen chamber-days left before the review on the 26th, eight ports to a block, four chamber-days to a block at most, the supply from 1.0 to 1.8 A, the chamber from the plan's own 85 °C up to 95 °C, seven spares of L-19 and five of L-22 in stores, the rig off for its service from 2026-03-14 to 2026-03-16, and the days coming off the schedule in order from 2026-03-09. A block the desk will not take comes back over the counter and the days are charged all the same. What is left of any of that is what the receipt stream leaves, not what the week started with.

A block earns its days by opening ground between the two laws that fit this build best: run it where the two of them say different things and the rows come back with an answer. It has to open at least half of what the best block the plan still allows would open. A block draws its ports from one lot and takes every port that lot can fill, up to the eight the rig holds, because the days are charged whatever is in the rig and a half-empty one buys half the rows for them. More ports at the drives and the case the campaign already ran open nothing at all, because the two laws agree there and the fresh spares come back on the sticker in the days a block has. The days Anders wants at 1.7 A are days of that kind. Stop only when the days left will not pay for the smallest block the desk takes, or no lot in stores can fill a port, or the schedule has no day left, or the day the schedule holds next falls inside the rig's service window, so there is no block the desk will take, or the range has already come inside the 350 hours the desk prints.

Every chamber-day the desk charged appears in the report once, with the block id the desk wrote, the drive and the case it was booked at, the ports it asked for, the modules and the bench rows it returned and the days charged. That includes the sheet that came back over the counter on the 9th: nothing ran on it and the two days went all the same, so they are spent and the report has to show them. A line the receipts do not carry is not a block. Say as well how many rows the figure was worked out of and how many modules they came from, split in the words `protocol.py` uses for it — the trace export, or a receipt — and remember that a reference read is not a module row.

`analysis.py` and `support.py` are the files to change. `protocol.py` is the shape the reliability reporting tooling reads a notebook through and ships with the tooling. The campaigns queued behind March bring their own modules, their own racks, their own meter and its sticker, their own catalogue constants, their own spares and their own chamber terms, so nothing in the notebook should be written for March's numbers.
