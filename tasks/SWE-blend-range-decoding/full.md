Finish the catalogue compositor so that the overnight export publishes the plate the design tool publishes for every shot and hands the studio back the files it opened. The recorded scenarios under `project_records/` are examples of what the export meets, not a list of the situations the finished compositor has to survive.

A shot whose layers store no range work, and a shot whose sliders all sit where the tool left them, publishes exactly the plate `protocol.plain_stack` publishes for it, pixel for pixel. A slider pair left alone weighs 1 everywhere: a dark slider with both grips at 0 keeps a pixel whose value is 0, and a light slider with both grips at 255 keeps a pixel whose value is 255, so the comparison at a grip includes the grip rather than excluding it. A layer whose `block` is `None` is drawn as it always was and never has a weight computed from an entry it does not carry.

The export re-writes the source files, so what a layer stored has to survive the trip through your reader and writer. For every layer, `bands.encode(bands.decode(layer.block))` is the stored words back again, value for value: every entry the layer carried, both grips of every slider packed the way `protocol.pack_handles` packs them rather than one byte copied into both halves, and `None` for a layer that carried no block rather than a full-range block invented for it.

Each stored entry carries two pairs of sliders and they are read against different things. The first pair — the first two words of the entry — is read against the layer's own value at that pixel. The second pair is read against what the plate already holds beneath that pixel: the running composite of every layer under this one at its place in the stack, which is neither the backdrop the shot started from nor the layer immediately below. Within each pair the first slider is the dark one and the second is the light one.

A dark slider keeps nothing below its left grip and everything from its right grip up; a light slider keeps everything up to its left grip and nothing above its right grip. Where a slider's two grips were dragged apart, the weight moves linearly between them — `(value - left) / (right - left)` rising for a dark slider and `(right - value) / (right - left)` falling for a light one — rather than stepping at either grip or at their midpoint. Where the two grips sit together the slider is that step. What every slider of every entry produces goes through `protocol.combine` into the layer's coverage weight, and the plate is written through `protocol.mix`, so the published plate is exact rather than approximate.

A layer's first stored entry is the grey one: it is read against `protocol.luminosity` of the layer's colour and of the plate beneath. Each entry after it belongs to one channel the layer declares, in declared order, and is read against that channel's value on the layer and on the plate beneath. The transparency channel's entry is stored and carried through the write-back but is drawn through nothing, because a pixel's transparency already reaches the plate through `protocol.mix`. How many entries a layer stores is a property of the file the studio saved rather than a count of its channels: a block a later release of the tool wrote runs past the last channel the layer declares, and an entry that addresses no channel this layer stores is carried through the write-back and drawn through nothing, rather than keying a channel it does not name or stopping the export.

`plate/bands.py`, `plate/compose.py` and `plate/support.py` are the files to change. `plate/protocol.py` ships with the design tool. You can inspect the project, replay the recorded scenarios and run your own experiments before you deliver.
