Finish the rating run's recovery region so that a rule that cannot price one order costs the night that one order and nothing else. The nights recorded under `project_records/` are examples of what the run meets, not an exhaustive list of the situations the finished region has to handle.

The billing ledger ends a night holding one branch's work for each order. An order the body priced carries the rows the body committed and nothing from `on_error`; an order the `on_error` branch priced carries that branch's rows and nothing the body committed on its way to failing; an order that reached no price at all — because its fault was not taken, because it ran out of attempts, because the step budget stopped it, or because the desk halted the run — leaves the ledger exactly as it found it. That means the `on_error` expression is evaluated only on the failure path, never in advance, because its rows would otherwise stand against every order the body priced cleanly; and it means the region takes a `mark()` on the way in and discards back to it before the fallback runs and before any fault leaves it.

A recovery region absorbs only what a rule body can produce: the faults gathered in `EVALUATION_FAULTS`. `StepBudgetExceeded` and `RunHalted` are the run's, not the order's, and both come straight out through the region, with the order left unpriced and the night's summary showing it as an order the run stopped rather than as one the rule raised something on. A region that catches them turns a runaway rule into a plausible discount on every order it touches and lets a night the desk has stopped finish with prices nobody has looked at.

Every rating reservation `hold()` takes inside a region is given back by the branch that took it, before the region moves on from that branch. For the body that is all four ways out of an attempt: a price, a fault that is going to be recovered, a fault or a signal that is leaving, and each attempt that is abandoned so that another can be tried. It is the same for the other two branches, which reserve on their own account: the `on_error` branch gives back what it took before the `cleanup` branch runs, and the `cleanup` branch gives back what it took before the region hands anything back. A branch that leaves its reservation to the end of the region has two of them open at once for one order the moment the next branch takes one, which is one more than the vendor's contract allows; a reservation no branch gives back is one the vendor bills by the hour for after the night has ended. A rule's `cleanup` branch runs once, however the region ends — on a price, on a recovered fault, and on a fault or a signal that is leaving — so an order that comes off the night with no price has been closed too, and what the branch raises replaces whatever the region was about to hand back, price included, with the order's ledger rows discarded with it. When the region hands back no price, the rows the closing branch itself wrote are discarded with the rest of that order's work: there is no price for finance to reconcile them against.

Re-execution is spent from the order's attempt budget, not the region's. `RunContext.attempts` counts every entry into a rule body for that order, wherever the body sits, so a region reached through an `on_error` branch draws on what the order has left rather than starting again. A body is retried while the rule is flagged `retry_on_error` and the order still has an attempt, and on nothing else: what the body raised does not decide it, and neither does the rule's `guard`, which says which fault the `on_error` branch is written for rather than whether the body is entered again. The attempt that reaches a price is the attempt the order is priced from, so a body that fails and then prices on a later attempt leaves that attempt's rows and its price behind and an earlier fault is spent; the `on_error` branch runs only once no attempt has reached a price. Once the retries are spent the `on_error` branch runs, so an order under a retrying rule the branch can price is priced by it rather than left over. When a body has to be entered and the order's budget is gone, the region raises `AttemptsExhausted` naming the order and the limit, and that outcome reaches the night's summary as itself: not as the last fault the body happened to raise, and not as a price from a branch reached with nothing left to spend.

The label an incident carries, and whether a rule's `guard` takes its fault, both follow the class the engine raised, which `ERROR_KINDS` enumerates, and never the wording of the message. A branch whose `guard` names the label of the fault in front of it takes that fault and prices the order from the branch; a fault whose label the `guard` does not name is not taken by that branch at all and leaves the region. Two orders that hit the same fault under the same rule are one incident on the morning board and both come out of that branch, however differently their messages read.
