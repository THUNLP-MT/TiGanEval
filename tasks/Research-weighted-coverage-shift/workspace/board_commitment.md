# What the arrival window promises, and what June cost

Agreed 2026-02-18 between Halstow dispatch, the contact centre and the regional
service manager. It applies to every job the depot dispatches, on every class.

## The promise

- Nine finishes in ten land inside the window printed on the card. That is the
  number the contact centre quotes and the number the depot is measured on.
- No window runs more than three hours end to end. A window wider than that is
  not an appointment and the centre will not read it out; the crew card then
  goes out with the tool's number bare and the job is booked as an all-day
  call.
- One window a month, set before the first of the month and not moved inside
  it.

For the audit desk's finite file, the cut must still make nine in ten when the
next job the card is being printed for is counted at the edge, not treated as
another row already behind the cut. A class needs at least nine completed
sheets before the desk will stand behind a window. The card uses the narrowest
symmetric whole-minute window that meets that promise; a wider window makes
customers wait longer without support from the file.

## The miss log

The contact centre logs every job whose finish landed outside the window it was
given. June: 1,842 jobs went out with a window on the card and 361 of them
finished outside it. Two of those were crews double-booked on the afternoon of
3 June, and one customer took a 40 credit.

The log is a count. It does not say which class a missed job was, because the
centre's ticket does not carry the class.

## Reporting

One board a month, off the same notebook, for this depot and for the others the
region runs. `protocol.py` is the shape the dispatch stack reads it through.
