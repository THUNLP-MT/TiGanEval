Complete the reminder schedule engine so that a rule written in wall-clock terms holds its wall-clock meaning everywhere the product uses it. The scenes recorded under `project_records/` are examples of what customers do, not an exhaustive list of the situations the finished engine has to handle.

Every occurrence of a rule lands on the wall-clock reading the rule was written with, in the rule's own zone, however many changes of offset the asked-for stretch crosses and in whichever zone the rule is written. Occurrences on either side of an offset change still render at the authored time of day; the change does not shift the meeting on the customer's clock. A `TIMES` count is a count of occurrences and a bounded series stops at the first occurrence at or after its end.

A wall-clock reading a zone stepped over, so that `local_offsets` gives no offset for it, still belongs to the series: it occurs, later on the clock by the length of the step, which is what resolving it against the offset that was in force before the step gives. A wall-clock reading a zone arrived at twice, so that `local_offsets` gives two offsets, occurs once and at the earlier of the two arrivals. Both readings still count towards a `TIMES` count, and neither is dropped and neither is emitted twice.

One meeting a customer holds through several feeds produces one reminder per moment. `merge_feeds` returns each absolute reading the feeds between them fire at exactly once, ascending, with nothing added and nothing lost, and the result does not depend on which zone any feed spells its copy of the rule in: a rule stored as 09:00 in `Europe/Berlin` and the same rule stored as the matching reading in `UTC` are the same reminder and are folded together.

The occurrences `expand` returns for a stretch are exactly the ones `Window.contains` admits, and the answers for consecutive pieces of a stretch are exactly the whole stretch's answer, split up: the dispatcher walking a night an hour at a time gets every reminder once and in order, with none appearing in two of the hours and none falling between them.

The answer for a stretch is settled by the rule and by that stretch, and by nothing else about the series. A meeting a customer has booked and left — a `TIMES` in the thousands, or an end years out — carries on past the end of the zone table, and the table refuses a reading it has no entry for rather than guessing an offset. Such a series still has an answer for every requested stretch inside the table's range; dates belonging only to the rest of the series must not make that request fail.

The same holds where the table's own range ends. A stretch that runs up to the far end of that range has its complete answer, whether the series is one a customer left running for years or an ordinary counted one. A later occurrence outside the table is not part of that stretch and must not prevent the occurrences inside it from being returned.

The text `serialize` writes is read back by `parse_rule_text` into the rule it was written from, and writing that rule back out again gives the same text. The start of a series is a time of day and carries its zone; the end of a bounded series is a moment and is written as one. A reading with neither a zone nor a `Z` is read back as absolute, and a reading carrying both is refused, so the two fields cannot be written the same way.

`recur/schedule.py` and `recur/support.py` are the files to change. `recur/protocol.py` is shared with the dispatcher, the mobile importer and the calendar view. You can inspect the project, replay the recorded scenes and run your own experiments before you deliver.
