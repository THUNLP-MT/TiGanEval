Finish the auto-clear readout and hand it back as a notebook the next queue can run unchanged. The council takes two things out of it: how often a listing we clear without showing it to anyone comes back violating, and where the cut should sit. Both are about the stream the router serves today.

That stream is the one in the traffic report's month-just-closed column, and the number has to be built at those channel shares. Two things in the workspace are not that stream and both look like it. The file behind the chart is the listings that came in under the earlier intake plan, so it is the marketplace the router served before the connector cutover, and the feed was a third of what it is now when those listings arrived. The intake log is not the mixture either: it is four months of arrivals either side of that cutover, so the export's own row counts carry the old mix as much as the new one. What the cutover moved is how much of each channel reaches us, not what the scorer does with a listing: the scorer artifact was not touched that day and has not been rebuilt since March. So a channel's rate is read over the rows the log holds for it whichever intake plan they came in under; the plan stamp is there to tell you what the mix used to be, not to split a channel in two. Read each channel on its own and put the channels back together at the shares the traffic report gives for the month just closed, whatever share of the log or of the batches we happen to hold for them.

Auto-cleared means the printed confidence was under the cut in force, which is on `context["review"]["cut_in_force"]` and is 0.06 here. The number is the share of those listings that came back violating, in points to one decimal, higher worse, and the council will act on it to within half a point.

A listing with an empty `review_outcome` has not come back clean; it has not come back at all. Count each channel only over the listings that carry an outcome. That matters here because it is not spread evenly: web and mobile close in a day or two, while the feed and the reseller import wait for the partner's Friday file, which reaches back six weeks on one and a month on the other, so the most recent stretch of those two channels is empty and the feed is the channel that grew. A listing a batch brings back is closed, and counts like any other written row.

Then the cut. Give the rate at the cut in force, and the loosest band on the sheet whose own rate — read the same way, over the same stream — still holds at or under the bar in `context["review"]["bar_points"]`, which is three points here. Where two bands hold, the answer is the looser one. Every cut is one of the ten bands in `context["review"]["bands"]`; nothing between them can be put in force.

Say how far the number could still move on the listings that carry an outcome, low endpoint first, and say it at a level that would have been safe at any of the five queues this readout is for: one chance in twenty across the run of them, not one in twenty per queue. Each channel moves by what its own adjudicated listings leave open, carried at that channel's share of the stream the same way its rate is, and the range the queue prints is the tightest one that still holds at that level once those movements are put together: the level is spent on that one range and not again on each channel's own movement inside it. Those movements are not laid end to end. They are different listings, closed by different reconciliations, so the channels do not all land at the far end of their own movement at once, and a range built by adding one channel's whole movement to the next is wider than the outcomes leave open. The one thing that is not a movement of that kind is a channel that has a share of the stream and nothing adjudicated behind it at all: nothing has been read on it and nothing narrows it, so the range has to stay open across the whole of that channel's share on top of whatever the channels that were read leave open. Nothing wider than that either: the traffic report is a count of the month rather than a draw from it, so there is nothing to add on top for how far the month itself might have landed. No tighter and no looser, because finance sets the reserve off it. Print how many adjudicated listings the number stands on.

For the next batch: the queue was granted 1,200 credits, 200 are charged, a batch takes at most 150 listings, and a listing costs two credits on the partner feed and on the reseller import and one on web and mobile. A cell that returns listings is charged for what it returns; a cell the desk works and finds empty is charged a flat twenty for the trip. A batch names one cell — one channel and one intake plan — and the desk cannot book one that leaves either out; it works that cell's backlog oldest first and returns whatever is in it, auto-cleared or not. What a cell is worth is the channel's share of the stream served now multiplied by the part of that channel's auto-cleared listings it would put an outcome against — counted over every auto-cleared listing the export holds for that channel, both intake plans together, and taking the whole of that cell's backlog rather than the slice one batch would bring back — so a channel that is already written through has nothing left to close, and a cell whose backlog holds no listing the cut cleared is worth nothing whatever else is sitting in it. Ask for the cell worth most, and for as much of it as the credits will carry. Stop only when no cell left holds forty listings the credits can pay for, and return that.
