Finish the 2027 Q1 reservation capacity readout and hand it back as a notebook the next quarter can run unchanged. The commitments desk takes one thing from it: how often the fitted model puts a quarter's peak wait over 2.5 seconds, and how far that could still move.

The commitment is written as a share of quarters, not as a duration, so the readout is about how often a quarter passes 2.5 seconds and not about where the middle of the draws sits. The middle is well determined and nobody is asking for it. Put the share in `answer` as a share of one, to three decimals, and the two ends of its range in `answer_range` with the low end first; both endpoints are shares, so neither can fall below zero or rise above one. The stored `peak_wait_s` column is in seconds and a step counts as a crossing when it is over 2.5.

The runs do not start on the surface the sampler is exploring. Each one comes down onto it over its own number of steps, and every one of those rows is stored and reads high. The sampler's proposal scale is still being moved while a run comes down and stops moving once the run is where it belongs, so `step_size` says on each file where that run's own opening block ends. Those lengths are not alike: `run-d` stored 300 such rows this quarter and `run-e` stored 90, and the resubmitted run has none at all. Take each run's own block off, and do not take a fixed number of rows off the head of every file — a count that fits this quarter throws away good draws on the next one and leaves bad ones in.

When `init_source` names another run and a stored step rather than a dispersed start, the two files are one walk and not two. The console puts a resubmitted job back on the last checkpoint the dead job wrote and re-walks what that job had already written before carrying on, so the replayed stretch stands in both files step for step. Here `run-f` was opened on `run-b` at step 1010 and the first 700 rows of `run-f` are `run-b`'s own rows over again. Keep one copy. Kept as two runs, that stretch is counted twice, and it is a congested one, so counting it twice pulls the share up. Read the overlap off the files rather than off this quarter's numbers: another quarter's resubmission starts somewhere else and replays a different amount.

Crossings do not arrive one at a time. A run sits in a congested stretch for a hundred steps or so and puts several crossings out while it is there, then runs hundreds of steps with none, so two steps close together say much the same thing and the draws carry far less about the share than their count suggests. Build the range on that and not on a count of steps. The range that goes under the number is the one that holds nineteen times in twenty, which is what the charter asks the group for and what the desk reads the renewal against. Work the width a second way as well, off segments of two or three hundred steps — a few times the length of one of those stretches, and enough of them that the segments themselves say something — and publish the wider of the two. A range worked as though every step said as much as the one before it comes out several times too tight and is not one the desk can use.

The review closes a quarter when the range is no more than 0.03 end to end and sits wholly on one side of the 5 per cent line. A range that reaches across the line leaves the question open however narrow it is, and the nearer end of it is not the answer. This quarter's draws support a range many times wider than 0.03, so the question is still open here.

What is left on the grant is what was granted, less the figure `compute_ledger.md` gives as spent at the last accounting, less every receipt on the stream — the jobs that were sent back included, because the console charges the queue slot whether or not it ran anything. On this quarter that is 24,300 granted, 6,710 already spent and 7,590 on the stream, which leaves 10,000.

A job the console will take names a run already on the manifest to carry on, or asks for a fresh dispersed start, and asks for at least 600 steps and at most 4,000. A job earns those steps when the range it leaves is no wider than the range a run started from scratch would leave if it were given the largest job the quarter can still buy — 4,000 steps, or whatever is left on the grant when that is less — and a fresh start pays a few hundred of its steps coming down onto the surface before it says anything. More than one job may clear that bar; the readout does not have to choose a unique best one. The resubmitted run does not clear it: it carries the scale of the job it was opened from and sits in those stretches longer than anything else in the quarter, so steps bought there come back saying what we already have.

Stop when the grant left will not pay for the smallest job the console takes, or when the range you publish already closes the question — the review reads that call off the range it is given, so a readout that prints a range which leaves the question open and then stops is one the desk cannot act on. Otherwise ask for a job that clears the information bar above.

Every number in the readout traces to a file or a receipt. List every run on the manifest exactly once with the steps it stored, the steps the readout read and the steps it set aside, and those two have to add up to what the run stored; the readout's own totals are the sums of those. List every receipt on the stream exactly once with the steps the console charged and whether it was sent back. A line neither the manifest nor the stream carries is not a measurement.
