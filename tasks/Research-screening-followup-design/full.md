Finish the plate review notebook and leave it in a state the bench can run unchanged on the screens queued behind this one. The design review takes two things out of it: how many kelvin the plate the lab picked takes off the module's case temperature rise against the spreader we ship today, and a range around that number. Head the report with the plate the lab picked out of the screen; the review is about that part and no other.

The first thing to know about this rig is that it does not read the same from one day to the next. Every rise read on a given day carries an amount that belongs to the day and not to the part, and that amount is as big as the differences between the plates the screen is trying to separate, or bigger. Every plate that ran on that day carries the same amount. The set point and ambient columns in the rig log are what the operator wrote down that morning; the day's amount does not follow from either of them, and no column in the table carries it. The reference plate is the proof that the amount is there and it is on your own table: one physical part, nothing touched between the two blocks it ran in, and its two scores sit the best part of a kelvin apart.

That is why a screen score is not the number the review wants. The score column is the stored incumbent figure of 18.4 K less the run's rise, and that stored figure was read on one day in May, so every score in the table is a difference between two days that were never the same day. Worse, the plate at the top of twelve is the plate whose day helped it most as well as the plate that is good, so the top score sits above what that plate is really worth by about as much as a day's amount, on top of whatever the characterisation day put into every score in the column. A rig day of the plate on its own against the stored figure does not fix it either, because that day has an amount of its own.

The number is what a rig day measures when both parts are on it. Book a day that runs the plate and the spreader we ship today, take the mean rise of each on that day, and the difference of the two means is what the plate is worth on that day's mounting, because the day's amount is in both means and drops out of the difference. There is no such day on the sheet today, so the number does not exist until you book one: run it, and the receipt has to be in the stream before the report stands. One day on its own is not enough either. The two mountings a day makes are that day's own, and a single day's difference can sit a third of a kelvin away from what the plate is worth, so book every rig day the sheet still holds and average the days' differences.

Two things are left between an averaged difference and the truth once the days are paired. One is how far a run sits from another run of the same part on the same day. The other is the mounting: the part is clamped once for the block it runs in and clamped fresh the next time, and every run of a block carries the interface it got, which is why two rig days of the same pair do not hand back quite the same difference. The days themselves measure both of those, so once there are two of them the range is about two standard errors of the mean of the days' differences, taken off the spread of those differences, low endpoint first. Never publish a range tighter than the runs inside the days already support: work the same two standard errors out a second way, from the run-to-run spread pooled over the blocks and the runs each day gave each part, and where that second reading is the wider of the two it is the one to publish. There is nothing to add for how far the rig moves between days, because pairing has already taken that out. Where no rig day carries both parts, the range has to carry the day-to-day move as well, and more than once over, since the stored figure sits on a day of its own.

Now the booking. Three rig days are left, a day holds four runs and two fixture changes, and the desk takes only parts on the shelf list. Every day you book carries both the plate and the spreader we ship, because a day with only the plate on it says nothing about the difference. Split the day's runs between the two parts so that the same sheet, run on each rig day that is left, gets the review a range inside the 0.85 K end to end that Hanna's note asks for; on this sheet that is the day's four runs split two and two, and a sheet that gives either part a single run does not get there. Stop when the sheet is empty, and stop as well once the range in hand is already inside what the note asks for.

Every rig day this review was charged for appears in the report once, with its booking id, the day the rig gave it and the runs it returned. That includes the day the desk handed the sheet back: nothing ran, and the day was charged all the same, so it is spent and the budget has to see it.

`analysis.py` and `support.py` are the files to change. `protocol.py` is the shape the reporting tooling reads a notebook through and ships with the tooling. The August mount-ring screen and the September fin screen run through the same two functions on their own plates, their own rig days, their own booking sheets and their own review notes, so nothing in the notebook should be written for July's numbers.
