Complete `iter_json` in `client/reader.py` as the readings collector's production JSON reader. It has to be correct for every body the site gateways send, honest about the ones it cannot read, incremental, and disciplined about the transport. The recorded traces under `project_records/` are examples of what the gateways have sent; they are not the list of responses the finished reader must handle. `client/protocol.py` and `client/wire.py` stay as they are; `client/reader.py` and `client/support.py` are yours.

**The value sequence, whatever size the transport hands the body over in.** For the `application/json` family the body is exactly one JSON text: a top-level array yields its elements one by one, in order, and any other JSON text is yielded alone. For `application/ndjson` and `application/x-ndjson` the body is lines separated by LF, CR, or CRLF, blank lines carry no value, and each remaining line is exactly one JSON text. For `application/json-seq` the payload opens on a record separator: its first character is one, and whatever sits in front of it — a stray newline, any other whitespace, the tail of a record written earlier — is the sign that the start of the payload never reached you, so it is refused rather than skipped past. Each record runs from its separator to the next separator or to the end of the payload, at most one trailing newline is stripped from a record, and a record between two separators that is empty after that is passed over; the last separator is different, in that what follows it to the end of the payload is the last record, and a last record that is empty once its trailing newline is off means the payload stopped in transit. One payload alone has no opening separator and is still read: a payload that is empty or holds nothing but whitespace from end to end, which carries no records at all. Whitespace here, and everywhere below, is space, tab, carriage return and line feed; a record separator is not whitespace. The same body must produce exactly the same values in the same order whether the transport delivers it one byte at a time, in small chunks that fall inside multi-byte characters, or in a single chunk.

**Refusing what cannot be read, without inventing anything.** A body carries a JSON value stream when its media type is one of `application/json`, `application/ndjson`, `application/x-ndjson`, `application/json-seq`, or any `application/<subtype>+json`; matching is against the whole media type, ignoring case and parameters, so nothing outside the `application/` tree qualifies however its subtype is spelled, and the `+json` suffix counts only inside that tree. Any other media type, and any body the reader cannot honestly decode, must raise `DecodingError`: an `application/json` body with no JSON text or with non-whitespace data after the JSON text, a body cut off part way, a `json-seq` payload that carries anything at all in front of its first record separator, whitespace included, and a `json-seq` payload whose last separator is followed by no JSON text. No value may be handed to the caller out of the part of the body that turned out to be broken; values already delivered before the failure point remain valid and in order.

**The declared or detected encoding.** Character encoding comes from the `charset` parameter when the header carries one, and a parameter naming a codec that does not exist is a `DecodingError`. Otherwise it comes from the leading bytes of the body, which may be UTF-8, UTF-16, or UTF-32 and may begin with a byte-order mark. Framing is applied to decoded text, not to raw bytes, and a leading byte-order mark belongs to the body rather than to the first value.

**Reading ahead no further than the collector allows.** When the caller receives a value, the reader must not have pulled meaningfully more of the body than that value's framing required: one transport chunk plus `READ_AHEAD_SLACK_BYTES` beyond it, never the whole body.

**Handling the transport exactly once.** A streaming response must be closed exactly once when its iteration finishes, when it fails, and when the caller abandons it after a partial read; it must never be read after closing, and a second iteration of an already-consumed streaming response must raise `StreamConsumed`. A response already held in memory must be re-readable and must not close anything.
