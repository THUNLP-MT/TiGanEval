The submission takes one number out of it: the pressure at which one in ten of the family leak, off the March build, under the hold the plan is written against, which is thirty minutes. It goes in in the gauge's bar and the reviewer's tolerance on it is five bar, with a range printed underneath.

The ladder is the six shared cartridges. A cartridge is one level whatever else changed around it, so a cartridge that was run in three batches carries the couplings of all three at that one level, and the level's leak fraction is the leaks over the couplings behind it. Across the levels the fraction climbs with pressure, and the log-odds of it run straight against pressure — that is the shape this family and every one behind it has always come back in. Put that straight line through the levels the ladder counts and read off the pressure where it reaches one in ten.

The console is not the gauge, and neither is the label on the cartridge: the number in a cartridge's name is the size the shop orders it by, and where a level sits is what that cartridge's own rows come back at once they are in the gauge's bar. Each bench writes the pressure it sees through its own factor, and the factor is what `DWB-200` says it is: one dead-weight block, certified at 200.0 bar, that goes on at the start and the end of every batch and whose rows are in the run table like any other row. Average a batch's two block readings, hold that against the block's own 200.0, and put every row of that batch back into the gauge's bar with it before the row joins a level. A chamber session carries its own pair of block readings on its receipt and is read the same way. Two batches on the same bench are still two batches; take each one's own pair.

Both consoles stop at the sticker on the front, which reads 203.0 bar. A row that came back at 203.0 says the coupling was at least there and does not say where, and three of the six cartridges did that on every unit of every batch. No factor recovers them: putting a 203.0 row back through its bench's factor turns one cartridge into two different pressures, one for each bench, and both of them are readings of the console rather than of the family. Those rows carry no level and are left out of the ladder.

The plan is written against thirty minutes and B-02 was held five. A coupling held five minutes is a different question about the same family, so B-02's rows sit at no level on this ladder whatever cartridge they name, and it is the hold column that decides that and not the cartridge label. More than half the couplings the qualification ran are in that batch, so leaving them in moves the number a long way.

The range is two standard errors of that pressure either side of it, taken off the same fit, and it is not to be printed tighter than the couplings the ladder counted support. In that fit every level counts for the couplings behind it and for how near its own fraction sits to a half: a level with more couplings behind it pins the line harder than a thinner one, and a level whose fraction is close to nought or one pins it less than one near the middle. There is nothing to add on top of that, because these are the couplings that were run rather than a draw from a larger run, and nothing to take off either, because the reviewer books the rating against the low end.

A session earns its hour when it puts couplings where the crossing is: at the plan's hold, at a cartridge the console gives a pressure for, at one of the two levels the crossing sits between on the ladder as it stands, with as many couplings on it as the ports and that batch will fill. A cartridge outside those two adds couplings to a part of the ladder that is already decided. The cartridge Ruth wants the hours on is one the console stops at, so an hour there returns 203.0 six times and buys no level at all. Stop only when the hours left will not pay for a session at the plan's hold, or no batch in stores holds a coupling, or the scheduler has no day left.

Every hour the desk charged appears in the report once, with the booking id the desk wrote, the bench, the hold, the couplings returned, the leaks among them and the hours charged. That includes the sheet that came back over the counter on the 8th: nothing ran on it and the hour went all the same, so it is spent and the report has to show it.
