Finish the January apportionment for the Block A header and leave the notebook in a state the next window can run unchanged. The report is about one thing: what share of the heat the Block A meter delivered between 1 and 31 January came off each of the three supplies feeding that header — the district tie-in, the gas plant and the data-hall recovery. Three fractions of that month's delivered heat, adding to one. Not fractions of the slots on the sheet, and not any other block or any other month.

The sample sheet is not the month. The contractor draws his round on the days he is on site, and the meter says those days are nowhere near equal: the 12th delivered two and a half times what the 4th did. So each slot speaks for the day it was drawn on, and a day counts for the kilowatt-hours the meter says it delivered that day; where a day gave you more than one slot you keep, they share that day's heat between them. Average the slots as if they were the month and you will hand the director the mixture of a mild morning.

Two of the slots are not this header at all. The board has a changeover on the plant-room set on 12 January between two and four in the afternoon, with the east riser valved out and the tie-in's interface set isolated, and the contractor drew two slots while it was on. Whatever was in the header for those two hours, it was not the three supplies this report is about. Set those two aside, and name them in the report so the director can see which slots stood behind the figures and which did not. Any slot drawn while an entry on the board is open goes the same way.

The characterisation sheet is one day's reading and the tie-in does not hold still. The operator doses makeup water off the outdoor sensor, so the tie-in's conductivity comes down as the weather does; the sheet was read on 4 January at 7.2 degrees, which was the mildest day of the window, and the month went to minus six. Take the sheet's tie-in figure as read and every colder slot puts the difference somewhere it does not belong. The rate is the drop between two readings of the tie-in station at two different outdoor temperatures, so it does not exist until the plant room has been read cold: a visit to `tie_in_plant_room`, on one of the February days the desk will serve, at least three degrees off the sheet's day. Nothing on the sample sheet gives it to you. The header's own rows do not either — the mixture moves with the weather at the same time as the signature does, so a slope fitted through the header's conductivity against the weather is a slope through both of them at once and is not the rate.

The marker is the only channel that tells the recovery return from the other two, and the recovery line on the sheet is `<1.0` with the floor flag on it. That is a bound and not a number: the analyser did not accumulate enough counts in five minutes to print a figure, so all it says is that the marker is somewhere below one. Every value it could be gives its own answer for the month. Print the figure at the middle of them and the band from one end to the other, low end first — and never narrower than that. Widen each end by two readings' worth of what the analyser's own note says a slot repeats to. That widening is what the repeatability leaves on the weighted figure, not what it leaves on one slot: the analyser lands somewhere new on every slot it draws, so the eleven slots the figures stand on leave less of it on the month than any one of them leaves on its own day, and how much of the figure each of them is carrying while it does that is what the day weights already say. The two channels are two reads of the same slot and both of them move the share. Then widen by as much again for what is left of the dosing rate once you have measured it: the two widenings go on one after the other, end to end, and are not folded into one as though they were two draws of the same thing. Where a sheet prints a figure on the recovery line instead of a floor flag, that figure is taken as read the way the other two rows are: there is nothing under the figure to run over, and the two widenings still go on. And once a thirty-minute riser slot has printed a marker for the recovery return, that reading is what is left to run over: from two readings' worth of the thirty-minute repeatability below the mean of those slots to two readings' worth above it, and never below nothing. The recovery share is the one the director will act on; give it a range and not a point. The stack replays a notebook against a window's own state, so the range it prints is that state's range: for the round as it stood in January, before anything was bought, it is the wider one, because nothing had read the marker then; for the same window with the plant room and the riser on it, it is the narrower one. A width that comes out the same at both is not a range these records carry.

One visit closes what the header cannot: a thirty-minute slot at `east_riser`, which reads the recovery return's own marker against the lower floor and prints a figure where five minutes prints a bound. The hire module is not a second way round it. Its third channel has no floor on it, but what a header slot taken with the module fitted gives back about the recovery signature is no better than the bound the sheet already carries, and it costs two slots on top of the visit it is fitted to. More header slots close nothing: the rounds the desk will still serve fall in February, after the window has closed, so a slot drawn on one of them is not a slot of this January.

For the booking: 14 slots are left of the 30 the agreement granted, and `sample_ledger.json` carries what every visit so far cost. A visit names one point and at most four slots; a thirty-minute slot counts as two, and fitting the hire module costs two on top. The access list is the header, the two plant rooms and the east riser, and a visit the desk hands back is charged all the same — the 26 January round to the boiler house was, and its three slots are gone. The desk serves one round a day on 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 February. Ask for the rate first, because until it is measured nothing else in the report stands up; then the recovery marker.

Stop when the range on the recovery share is inside the two in a hundred either side the director asked for and the rate has been measured, or when nothing the slots left can still buy would tighten the answer. Not before: a stop with the recovery range still wide and slots on the agreement is the answer the billing clause cannot use. The range that clause reads is the one the slots themselves leave — the sweep over what the marker could still be, widened by the analyser's own repeatability — taken before the dosing term goes on. The dosing term is what the plant-room round buys back, and the rate having been read is already the other half of the clause; reading it into the range as well charges the same round twice.

Every visit the ledger carries appears in the report once, with its call id, its point and the slots it cost, the round the desk handed back included. A line the ledger does not carry is not a visit. And every slot on the sample sheet inside the window appears once too, either among the ones the figures stand on or among the ones set aside.

`analysis.py` and `support.py` are the files to change. `protocol.py` is the shape the estates reporting stack reads a notebook through and ships with the stack. The same notebook runs February and March on their own sheets, and Blocks B and C on theirs, each with its own characterisation day, its own dosing rate, its own board and its own grant. The stack has picked up a building on another main since, and that one is nothing like January: a month that never turns cold, a board with no changeover on it anywhere, and a recovery return carrying enough marker that its own line on the characterisation sheet prints a figure rather than a floor flag. The recovery signature there is already known, so there is nothing under it to run over and nothing a riser slot could close, and the plan there is a stop even with slots in hand and days the desk would serve. Nothing in the notebook should be written for January's numbers.
