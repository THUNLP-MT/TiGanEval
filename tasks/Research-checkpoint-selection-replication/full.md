The board takes two things out of it: the checkpoint we are asking it to promote, by the id the registry uses, and what that one checkpoint gains over `pr-rank-2024q4` as it runs in production now, in points of `val_score` where one point is 0.01, with a range under it, low end first. It is one checkpoint against one model. Not an average over a run, not an average over a shortlist, and not a figure about the run that produced the checkpoint.

The first thing to know about the table is that a score carries the lot it was read on as well as the checkpoint. Each lot has a level of its own, and the two this campaign ran on sit further apart than anything that separates the checkpoints we are choosing between: r01 to r04 finished before the rota moved the default on 17 August and went through the automatic evaluation on `v-2f9c`, r05 to r08 finished after and went through on `v-8b41`, and the rows of the second group read higher for that reason before any checkpoint of theirs is better. `val_lot` is the column that says which lot a row sat on; `trainer_version` changes in the middle of the second group and says nothing about it. And the figure the release note stores for production, 0.7818, was read in March on `v-1c07`, which was retired in June and cannot be scored again, so it is on neither of today's lots and it does not compare with anything we read now.

The second thing is the top of the table itself. It is the largest of about three hundred and sixty reads, so the row that tops it is the row whose lot, whose pass of that lot and whose own luck all ran the same way, and it sits above what that checkpoint is worth by more than anything that separates the checkpoints we are choosing between. Whatever the number for the board is, it is not that row less anything.

The number is a desk job with a checkpoint and `pr-rank-2024q4` in it, on one lot, and it does not exist until there is a receipt for it. The desk scores every item in a job on the same passes of the lot the job names, so a difference taken inside one job has the lot and the pass out of it at both ends; a read of a checkpoint from one job set against a read of the model from another does not, and is a difference between two lots as much as between two things. A lot is eight passes, served in order, and the desk will not sell a ninth of the same one, so what a job may ask for on a lot is the passes of it the desk has not served yet. Three reads apiece is enough to put a shortlist in order without spending a whole lot on rows that will not be shipped, and `pr-rank-2024q4` goes into that first job with them, so the same passes both rank the shortlist and count towards the number; a job that reads candidates against each other with the model left out spends passes of the lot that no later job can buy back. The number itself wants the rest of that lot spent on the row that came out ahead, because what is left inside a job is each item's own luck on the rows it happened to get and that only comes down by reading more of the lot.

The range is two standard errors of the mean of those paired differences, worked off the spread of the differences themselves, low end first, with the signed number between its ends. Never publish one tighter than that spread supports. The promotion rule leaves a packet pending when the low end reaches zero gain or below, and platform reopens one when the signed centre and the later smoke readout sit more than four tenths of a point apart. Where nothing on the stream puts a checkpoint and the model on one lot together, the reading crosses from one lot to another, the move between lots is in it at both ends, and the range has to carry that move twice over, which is far wider than any pair of reads would give.

Which checkpoint goes in the readout is settled by the fresh reads and not by the table, so once anything has been read the readout names one of the rows a job put on a lot beside `pr-rank-2024q4` and never a row the desk has not scored. A row chosen for promotion also has to leave its range above zero and inside the board's ask. A row read three times and a row read eight times are not on the same footing, so among rows that can be promoted the row the readout names is the one the lot was actually spent on — the row with the most paired reads — and where two rows read the same number of times sit inside each other's range the earlier checkpoint is the one to ship. Two runs were on the long warm-up and turn down after their turn step, r03 after 15,000 steps and r07 after 18,500, and their own curves under `curves/` show it while the ledger rows do not say so. Those rows were measured honestly and they stay in the table; what is true of them is that the late checkpoints of those two runs are worse than their early ones, and one post-turn row still sits high enough up the table to be picked up as a candidate.

For the next job: the campaign has 900 units left of the 1,030 it was granted, and 150 of those are the release smoke check, which is not the campaign's to spend. Platform caps a single job at 480 units and a read costs 30, so a job names what it can pay for out of the 750 that are free. Only checkpoints the registry still shows on disk can be scored; the retention job took 229 of the 360 and a job that names one of them comes back unrun with the 10-unit queue fee charged against the same grant. A job worth sending puts at least one checkpoint the registry still shows on disk and `pr-rank-2024q4` on one open lot together, inside the grant, the reserve and the cap, and asks for no more passes of that lot than the desk has left. A training run costs 520 and buys no reading of any of this; the eight runs behind this campaign were billed to the training grant and the desk hands a training job back unrun against this one, with the queue fee charged, so it is not what the units left are for. There are two stops and no third. The first is that what is left will not pay for a job at all. The second needs both halves at once: the lot behind the number has been read out, and the range in hand is already inside the one point the board asks for. A range inside that point with passes of that lot still unsold is not a stop, because a spread worked off three differences is a reading of three rows' luck as much as of the spread; a lot read out with the range still wider than the ask is not one either. A stop leaves the smoke check alone whichever way it comes.

Every job the receipt stream carries appears in the readout once, with its job id, the lot it ran on, the reads it returned and the units it charged, the refused ones included, and the units the readout says are left have to be the grant less everything the stream was charged. A line the stream does not carry is not a job.
