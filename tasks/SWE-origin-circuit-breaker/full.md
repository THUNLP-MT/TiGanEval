Complete the edge gateway's outbound call path so that one unwell upstream stops the fleet calling that upstream and changes nothing else. The stretches of traffic recorded under `project_records/` are examples of what the gateway sees, not an exhaustive list of the situations the finished call path has to handle.

The health of an upstream is held per origin, and one map of it is shared by the base client and every client built from it with `create`. The origin is the one `resolve_origin` gives for the request as it will actually be sent: after a relative target has been joined to the client's `base_url`, whatever shape the caller passed — an absolute URL, a path, a `Url`, or a `Request` another layer built — and distinguishing scheme, host and port, so two upstreams on the same host at different ports are two upstreams. Failures a fleet-wide upstream shows to one client are therefore known to all of them, and an origin nothing has failed at is never refused because a different origin is unwell.

One logical call contributes exactly one settlement to its origin, whatever the request's `retries` budget makes it do inside itself. The admission decision is taken once, for the outermost call, and carried through the internal attempts rather than taken again; the outcome is settled once when the call finishes. A client's `threshold` therefore counts caller-visible calls and not attempts: the call whose failure makes the streak reach it is the one that takes the origin out of reach. When a failing call opens an origin, the moment it is dated from is the clock reading at which its last attempt failed, so the wait a caller sees begins when the origin last actually failed the fleet.

Recovery is admission-controlled. Once `cooldown_ms` has passed since the origin last failed, every waiting call is let through while fewer than `half_open_max` calls for that origin already hold a recovery place; only calls that arrive when all those places are occupied are refused straight away, exactly as they are during the wait. A call that has been let through holds its place for the whole logical call, including its internal attempts, and gives it back on every way out: a success, a counted failure, a status that was merely rejected, and an exception raised out of the caller's response transform. A call that finds out the origin is still unwell puts it back out of reach, dated at that failure.

Outcomes are read three ways, through the frozen `classify_outcome`, and the three do different things. An attempt with no response, a body the caller's transform could not read, and a status in `FAILURE_STATUSES` are failures: they lengthen the streak and can take the origin out of reach or put it back out of reach. A status outside that list is a rejection: it gives back any place the call held and returns without touching the streak, the state or the moment the origin was taken out of reach, because the origin answering that a charge does not exist says nothing about the origin's health. Only a genuinely successful call resets the streak and brings the origin back. The reading comes from the response and the error as they arrived, before `ignore_errors` decides what this one caller is handed, so a listed status counts for a caller that asked for error responses back exactly as it counts for one that did not.

A refused call is inert. It reaches no transport, changes no failure count, moves no timestamp and consumes no place among the calls being let through. That is what makes the wait end: traffic arriving while an origin is out of reach must not push the moment it was taken out of reach forward, or steady traffic keeps a ten-minute outage going for ever. A request that did not set `guarded` is outside all of this — it is never refused and never recorded, because the platform's health probe and the internal bookkeeping calls are how the fleet finds out that an upstream came back.

`gateway/breaker.py` and `gateway/support.py` are the files to change. `gateway/protocol.py` ships with the other gateways in the platform release. You can inspect the project, replay the recorded traffic and run your own experiments before you deliver.
