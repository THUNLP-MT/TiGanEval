Finish the Ryecourt build note and hand it back as a notebook the next region can run unchanged. The board takes one decision from it: the 2022-H2 build comes off the fleet early, or it stays and runs its term.

Ryecourt is three depots, Kestrel, Ardmore and Sallow, and twenty-four monthly returns closing on 31 March 2024. A van belongs to one of those months when it was on the fleet in that month, and to the depot that held it then.

The number is faults per hundred vehicle-months, the 2022-H2 build minus the 2021-H2 build at one age, to one decimal place, positive meaning worse. The board takes it if it lands within 0.4 of what the returns say and sends the paper back if it does not.

Count the vehicle-months as days on the fleet. A van earns its month from the day it went into service through the day it went back, and a part month is the part of it the van was there for. If it moves, `transfer_date` is the first day the receiving depot holds it: count that day once, at the receiving depot and not at the old one. Count them over the twenty-four returns and no further. A depot only raises faults on a month the returns cover, so a month outside them comes back with none: the 2021-H2 vans were running months before the first return and are still running after the last one, the 2022-H2 order was handed over after the window opened and went back before it closed, and a notebook that counts a van's whole life loads the older build with running that no return ever reported against it. `depot_roster.csv` is not that: the depot puts a van on its roster when the van is allocated to it, weeks before hand-over, and keeps it there until the leasing company has collected it back, weeks after hand-back, and it counts heads on the first of the month rather than days in it. A headcount is not a month.

The whole of the 2022-H2 order went out on a fourteen-month lease and went back at fourteen months, so it is on the returns young and never old, while the 2021-H2 vans are on them at twice that age. A number read straight off the two of them as they stand is a number about two different age ranges, not about the build.

Kestrel raised its faults on the 30-point walkaround card up to 31 August 2022 and on the 52-point card from 1 September; Ardmore and Sallow never moved off the 30-point. The longer card books more on the same van, and most of the 2022-H2 build sits at Kestrel in exactly the months it was young, so the two cards have to go on one scale before anything is added up. Read what the longer card is worth on the vans both cards saw: the same build months in the same return months, at the depot that moved and at the depots that did not, weighted the same on each side, and put the longer card's counts back on the 30-point scale by it. A region whose depots never changed card has nothing to put back.

A van's age is its return month less its build month, so age, calendar month and build month move together and the returns on their own do not say which of the three a difference belongs to. A build read off what a fit on age and calendar month leaves over is not reading the build; it is reading how that fit happened to split them. What separates them is the build sheets. A change order takes effect in a build month and stands until the next one, so the build months between two orders came off the line to one body spec and one set of lots and carry one build number — no change order ran across March and April 2021, and what separates those two build months is not the build. Hold the build months of one standard to one number and the three come apart.

So: over cells of build month by return month, fit faults per hundred vehicle-months on one level, one slope in months of age, one term for each return month and one term for each build standard, weighting each cell by the vehicle-months in it. The answer is the 2022-H2 standards minus the 2021-H2 standards, each build month counted by the vans it put on the road. The `vehicle_months` line is service-day exposure inside the return window represented by the note: either the whole fitted table or the two named build groups whose contrast it reports, never roster heads or months outside the returns.

The range goes low end first and contains the reported build difference. It has to be safe read at any of the four regions this note goes to, one chance in twenty across the four, so it is the spread the fit leaves around that difference at that level. That spread is what the vehicle-months behind the answer support, and it is the one width the range is measured against: the range cannot be tighter than it, and it must not be more than four times that. The one-point bar in `fleet_review_note.md` is what the board does with the range once it has it, not a width to build the range to: a range wider than a point is the board being told that we do not yet know, and it is still the range the vehicle-months support.

For the next day: the audit has eighteen inspector days and three are charged. The inspector works weekdays, no more than four days in any Monday-to-Sunday week, no day twice, no more than twenty-five vans a day, and only at the three depots on the plan. A day is booked against exactly two build groups at one depot inside one age band, and the depot has to hold ten or more vans of each of those groups in service that day; below that the charge hand hands the day back and it is charged all the same. What a day is worth is the card it reads: the audit has spent its three days at Kestrel and has never put an inspector in front of the 30-point card, and a day at a depot whose card a day has already read buys nothing. A day the depot handed back read nothing, so the card it was booked against is still unread. Stop when every card on the region's roster has had a day on it, when no depot can make up a day, or when the days are gone, and say so.

Every day the desk has charged appears in the note once, with its receipt id, the depot, the day, and the vans and faults the inspector came back with, the days a depot handed back unworked included, and the note's own total of days charged is the desk's. A day the receipts do not carry is not a day.
