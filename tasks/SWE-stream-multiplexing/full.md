Finish the station's end of the telemetry uplink, so that one radio link carries several conversations with a depot unit at once and each of them behaves as it would on a link of its own. The drills under `project_records/` are examples of what the link does, not an exhaustive list of the situations the finished session has to survive.

Everything the session puts on the radio decodes under the frozen codec into frames the unit's firmware accepts, so `protocol.PeerEnd` refuses nothing the session sends. A stream is opened with an `OPEN` frame before any payload goes out on it, no frame carries a payload larger than `SessionConfig.max_frame_size`, and stream ids are allocated so that they can never collide with the ids the far end allocates for the streams it opens itself: both ends open streams on the same link, the unit takes its own ids from one run of numbers, and the station has to take its from another.

Every payload byte a caller hands a stream reaches the unit's matching stream once, in the order it was written, and every payload byte the unit sends comes back out of the matching stream on this side once and in order, whatever the slot sizes and the interleaving of the traffic. The six numbers in `LinkCounters` describe that link and not the session's intentions: `payload_bytes_sent` and `payload_bytes_received` are `DATA` payload that went over the radio, counted where a frame is emitted and where one arrives rather than where a caller handed bytes over, and never including frame headers or the payload of control frames; `frames_sent` and `frames_received` are every frame either way, control frames included; and `streams_opened` counts each stream on the link once, whichever end opened it, against `streams_closed`, which counts it once when it leaves `session.streams`.

No more payload stands unread at a unit than that unit has room for. A stream's outstanding payload — what has been put on the radio for it and not yet taken by the unit's consumer — never exceeds `SessionConfig.send_window`; a stream with no room left keeps what it still owes and sends it when the unit's `GRANT` frames give the room back, so a transfer several times larger than the window still finishes rather than deadlocking or overrunning the unit.

One stream whose consumer has stopped taking bytes does not stop any other stream on the link. No call to `pump(max_bytes)` puts more than `max_bytes` on the radio. Where a drill requires the other streams to finish, its slots provide enough wire capacity for legal framing, control traffic and the stalled stream's remaining window; under that premise, every other stream that has payload to send and room at the far end reaches the end of what it owes, whichever stream stalls, whatever its service class, and wherever it sits in the order the streams were opened. When a slot opens on several streams that could be served in it, the first payload that slot carries belongs to the most urgent of them: a slot never begins by serving a lower-priority stream while a stream of a higher class had payload to send and room at the far end when the slot opened. How the rest of that slot is shared is not prescribed, provided the completion and window rules still hold.

A stream comes down from both ends and takes nothing with it. Closing a stream here stops this side writing, signals that half of the close with one `SHUT` when the link is next given room for the control frame, and leaves whatever has already arrived on it readable until it has been drained; a stream leaves `session.streams` only once both ends have closed it and nothing is left unread on it. Closing a stream, learning that the far end has closed it, or closing the link finishes every write still owed on it and marks it with `protocol.ClosedLink` rather than leaving it waiting for room that will never come; writing to a stream either end has closed, or to a closed link, and opening a stream on a closed link, raise `ClosedLink`. `session.close()` makes a best-effort close notification, shuts the link down and returns even when the radio will not take another byte.

`link/session.py` and `link/streams.py` are the files to change. `link/protocol.py` ships with the depot firmware. You can inspect the project, replay the recorded drills and run your own experiments before you deliver.
